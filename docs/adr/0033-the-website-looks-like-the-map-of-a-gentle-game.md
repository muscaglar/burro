# 0033. The website looks like the map of a gentle game, and reading comes first

Status: accepted, 2026-09-26. The founder settled the look that day, after seeing two earlier accounts of it on the website itself. What was decided is the founder's. The figures the look was built with, and every call of taste, are the founder's to overturn: [the design of the look](../design/look.md) gives each with the one line that turns it. The iPhone app is not given the look ([0037](0037-the-iphone-app-is-held-to-a-copy-of-the-websites-colours-and-words.md)). **Amended the same day, once the founder had walked the website**: the rabbit moves all the time and can be stopped, no edge is dashed, and nothing beside what Burro understood says who read the words. See "Amended, 2026-09-26", below, which says what in this record it changes. **Amended again that evening, once the founder had walked the website a second time**: the rabbit has no button, and nothing stops him but a system that asks for less movement, a first search waits a few seconds with him, and nothing under the box says who reads the words. See "Amended again, 2026-09-26", below. **Amended a third time on 2026-09-27, once the founder had walked the website a third time**: every gauge has a picture at each end, one at each, and neither end is the better one; recorded crime is drawn as a count that was written down; what an area gives up is drawn as one thing weighed against another, and nothing is red to say danger; and the rabbit hops more slowly and for longer, and throws soil. See "Amended a third time, 2026-09-27", below.

## Context

- The website had no look of its own. It was set in the faces of the system, on white, and turned dark when the system did.
- Several looks were drawn, each a whole look built as a page. Every one was drawn round a donkey, because "burro" is the Spanish for one. Nobody had asked what the name means. The founder then said it: "a play on words for the London Borough and a Rabbit burrowing into their home".
- The look that was built first was one of them, in browns and wood. It was then held back, to paper and ink with a drawing here and there. The founder saw both on the website, and chose another of the looks that were drawn, in full: "Bring the town map look, feel and gamified elements to the build now." Town Map is the map of a gentle game, drawn in pixels. [The history](../history.md) has the day.
- Burro is for adults who choose where to rent or buy. The regulator names cartoons, animation and gamification among what draws children to a service, and the terms of the model that reads what is typed forbid a product that children are likely to use. [The page on children](../legal/access-by-children.md) weighs it. The founder decided that the look stands, and said of what a regulation may ask: "we can remove things or downplay things in relation to regulation later if needed". So every part of the look is built to be turned down in one line.

## Decision

**The website has the look of Town Map, on the pages as they work. What a press does and what is said are as they were.**

| Matter | What is so |
|---|---|
| The colours | Seventeen, each a token by its name: `ink`, `page`, `lea`, `sand`, `meadow`, `verge`, `hedge`, `shallows`, `haven`, `cobalt`, `amber`, `poppy`, `heath`, `fur`, `shade`, `slate` and `earth`. Three more, `dew`, `fern` and `ivy`, are greens of the map and of nothing else: five bands that are each 1.35 from the next, under a line that is 3 from every one, cannot be had from the seventeen. No style sheet writes a colour out, and no drawing holds any other |
| What each is for | Ink for words, rules and outlines. Cobalt for the one button that matters most in sight, and for links. Amber for what is on or chosen, and for the band of a notice. Poppy for the edge of a fault and the mark of a trade-off. Brown, which is `fur` and `earth`, is in the rabbit and his burrow and nowhere else. **Amended on 2026-09-27**: poppy is the mark of no trade-off, and nothing is red to say danger |
| What is read on | Cream, which is `page`, whatever a part calls its ground. Every sentence, figure and link stands in a box of it. Nothing is read on the grass. On `sand` ink alone is read. Poppy and amber are never the colour of words: on cream they are 3.4 and 1.6 to 1 |
| The ground | The meadow of Town Map's map in tiles, from edge to edge, under every page: one picture of twelve tiles each way, laid at two pixels of the screen to one of its own. It goes with the page as the page is scrolled |
| The two faces | Jersey 10, which is drawn in pixels, for the name of the website, the name of an area, a heading and a short label. Rubik for every sentence and every figure, every source, date and sum of money. Each is a file of the website's own, served from its own origin under the SIL Open Font License, version 1.1, with its licence beside it. Neither licence reserves a name. No face, no drawing and no style comes from another origin |
| Why Rubik | The founder chose it by eye, with three faces side by side on the website itself. Atkinson Hyperlegible, which Town Map was drawn in and first built in, draws a nought with a stroke through it, and a page of rents and prices is a page of noughts. Rubik draws a nought plain, and sits well beside the face of names |
| What was given up with it | Atkinson Hyperlegible was drawn for readers with low vision, so that letters which are taken for one another are told apart. In Rubik a capital I and a small l are two plain strokes, and a nought is told from a capital O by being narrower. Rubik is 5 in 100 wider, so a result is a line longer. Its slanted file holds one weight, so a slanted word is never heavy. It is served for the letters of western Europe alone, as the face before it was |
| One look | There are no dark colours. No style sheet asks the system which it prefers, and the browser is told that the page is light and only light |
| The box | The one frame of the look: cream, a double rule of ink with a notch at each corner, and a hard shadow down and to the right. The rule is a picture cut in nine, and under it is a plain edge of ink. What is many and small, as the rows of a table are, stands within a plain edge and has no shadow |
| A drawing | Written as text, one character a pixel, in `apps/web/art/`. A command makes the pictures and the list of their names from the text: `npm run gen:art`. Both are committed, neither is edited by hand, and the same text gives the same bytes on every machine. It is plain Node and adds no package |
| Which drawing stands for a thing | The one named for the id the service gives a vibe, then the one for its family, then the plain one. A drawing is dress. The name beside it is the service's, and no name of a vibe, a measure or a place is written into the website. No drawing holds a word or a figure, and none pictures a person |
| A rank | A pennant with its number, the same for the first result as for the tenth |
| Movement | Three things move, each for a reason a person can name. The rabbit hops while a person waits for a first answer, and stops when the answer is in. A chip that was added drops into its place in four steps, once. The face of a button steps into its shadow under a press. The length of each is a token, and is nought unless the system says that movement is welcome. What moves, moves in held frames and is never eased. Nothing but the rabbit moves without end. **Amended on 2026-09-26**: the rabbit moves all the time, wherever he is drawn, and a button stops him. **Amended again that evening**: he has no button, and a first search waits a few seconds so that he is seen to hop. **Amended a third time on 2026-09-27**: he hops more slowly, a first search waits longer, and soil flicks up from his hole |
| A press | Nothing moves or changes size under a press, the pointer or the focus: a press lands where it was aimed. One thing is let through. The face inside a button may step, by no more than its shadow is wide, under a press alone. The button itself, which is what takes the press, stays where it is and as large as it is |

**Burro is a rabbit**: a wild one of the British countryside, brown and grey. He is never a donkey, and nothing of a donkey's is drawn. **Amended on 2026-09-26**: where the table below says that he is still, he moves, unless he was stopped or the system asks for less movement. **Amended again that evening**: nobody can stop him, the system alone stills him, and its last row is no longer so: Burro asks nothing, so he stands beside no question.

| Moment | He | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Before a search | Sits, still | Beside the heading of the first screen |
| While a search is read, or a first ranking that a control asked for is worked out | Hops in and out of his hole, until the answer is in | Where the results are about to stand, in a box that is of one size whether he moves or not |
| The same, where the system asks for less movement | Sits up beside his hole, still | The same place |
| Burro is not sure, and asks | Stands on his hind legs, still | Beside the head of what he asks |

He is drawn nowhere else: not on a result, in the list, on the map, on the page of an area or beside a failure. Drawn beside one area he would say which area Burro thinks is home, and Burro gives no verdict of a place. He says nothing to a screen reader: what happens is said in words, as it was. His head is the icon of the website, which a browser draws beside the title of every page. It is no part of any page. **Amended on 2026-09-26**: he is drawn in what every page stands in, and so on the page of an area too, though beside no area of it. "Amended again, 2026-09-26", below, says where.

**What holds all of it**, however fully the look is built:

1. **Reading comes first.** Every sentence and every figure is in the reading face, in ink, on cream. The game is in the ground, the boxes, the buttons and the drawings, and never in the words.
2. **Nothing is won.** A vibe is a taste and never a score. No points, no stars, no level, no prize, no streak, nothing to collect, and no picture that says one area has beaten another.
3. **Nothing is drawn that the data does not hold.** No hill, no boat and no building on the map of a real city. The map is the real border of every area. A grain on the land is a grain, and says nothing.
4. **The answer comes first.** Where the look and that rule disagree, the look gives way ([0035](0035-the-search-page-is-one-box-and-the-look-gives-way-to-the-answer.md)).

What was weighed, and put aside:

| Way | Why not |
|---|---|
| The look as it was first drawn: a donkey with his panniers, twenty browns, frames of wood | The name means a rabbit and a borough. All that was the donkey's went, and a test refuses a drawing named for any of it |
| Paper first: the look held back, with a drawing as seasoning | It was built, and on the website it read as any quiet website. The founder asked for the fuller look |
| Atkinson Hyperlegible, or Lexend | The founder chose Rubik by eye, for its nought |
| A dark look beside the light one | Every pair of colours would be checked twice and every drawing drawn twice. It is not in this build |
| Every word in the face that is drawn in pixels, as Town Map set them | At 20 pixels it reads as small as a sentence at 13. A figure that must be read exactly is not set in it |
| Sand as the ground of a card or a panel | On sand a quiet word is 4.4 to 1, a link 4.1 and the green of what is good 3.4, where 4.5 is asked. Thirty parts lay words on that ground |
| A button that itself moves under a press, as Town Map's does | The press that follows would land on what stood under it. The pressed button is a second picture on the same canvas |
| A carrot that nudges, water that glints in steps, lines that type themselves out | Each moves without end, or to be looked at |
| A medal, a crown or gold for the first result | Nothing is won |
| Burro at home on the page of every area | He is drawn where a person waits or is asked, and nowhere a place is shown |
| Pictures kept as files that are drawn by hand | A change to a picture could not be read as a change to a line, and nothing would hold a picture to the colours of the look |

## Consequences

- **Every page is taller.** A box keeps room for its rules and its shadow, a drawing is three pixels of the screen to one of its own on a wide screen, and a name is 30 pixels and more. After a plain search the first result is whole on the first screen of a phone with 16 px to spare, and a results page on a desk is 4,562 px, where the design allowed 2,700. [The design](../design/web.md), sections 4.1 and 4.2, has what was measured.
- **The look is on the regulator's own list of what appeals to children.** That is weighed on [the page on children](../legal/access-by-children.md), which asked for this record. What holds the look is now held by tests, and the list below names them.
- **The address of a drawing names what it is of.** A chip or an offer of a vibe asks the website's own host for `/art/thing-leafy.png`. So the log of whatever serves the website says which vibes a search holds, and that it holds a journey or a budget: not where, and not how much. No word that was typed is in any address. It is a part of what a search holds all the same, and the website holds that a search is in no address ([0011](0011-nothing-is-kept-for-a-search.md)). Whether it may stand is the founder's to decide.
- **The statement of accessibility says what is so now**, and a test holds the figures it names to the style sheets. The statement went on the evening of 2026-09-26, and its tests with it ([0042](0042-the-website-makes-no-claim-of-a-standard-of-accessibility.md)).
- **No dependency was added.** The command that makes the pictures, and what reads the file of a face in the tests, are written with what Node brings.
- **Nobody has watched anything move.** The rabbit, a chip and a button were each looked at in still pictures, frame by frame, in one browser on one machine.
