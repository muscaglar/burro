# The words of the website

This guide is for anybody who writes a word that a visitor reads, whether that is the name of a button, the line under the search box or a paragraph on the page of vibes. It says who reads the website, how to write for them, and which promises a sentence has to keep however well it reads. It is written in the way it asks you to write, so that you can use it as an example as well as a set of rules.

The guide is the founder's direction. On 26 September 2026 the founder walked through the website in a browser, once it had been given its look, and wrote this about its words:

> Much of your copy in vibes is nonsensical, can you write copy in a manner that is more typical for a broad audience, dont default to short snappy sentances at the cost of readibilty and understanding. Dont add random summary sub heading unless it is actually additive to the user. Remember to add sentance structure like, this means, and because of that, etc

The words were written again to it, and the founder walked the website a second time that evening. What they wrote then is as much a part of this guide as what they wrote first, and it pulls the other way:

> on deep search you have duplicated the explainer text, remove the explainer copy from the settings section

> remove how your words are handeld from the home page search. this is overkill

> remove the concept of rough guide, we don't want to pass this on to a user

> remove the 'each little town' disclaimer on the ranking cards.

Every sentence they took away was written as this guide asks, and every one was true. Each was one sentence too many where it stood.

The founder walked the website a third time, late that night, and wrote of a result, which is one area of a ranking as it is first shown:

> remove the explainer text under the gauges. we dont need that level of detail. just show the incomplete data icon and simple text of 'approx data'

> Remove the why it fits section, again too much detail

> Again, we don't need to over complicate and bloat with too much info.

This time the words that went stood beside a drawing that said the same thing. A gauge is five steps with a peg on one of them. Beside it the website said "around the middle", and under it, where Burro could measure only part of what a vibe needs, a sentence said how large a part. The founder kept the gauge, asked for a picture at each end of it, and took the words away.

So the guide asks three things of a sentence: that it is written to be understood, that it is needed where it stands, and that no drawing beside it says the same thing already. "Say it once, and where it helps", below, has the second and the third.

[ADR 0038](../adr/0038-the-words-of-the-website-are-written-for-a-person-who-has-never-seen-burro.md) records what was decided and why. This page is the one you work from.

The guide is about the words a visitor reads on the website. It asks nothing of the other documents of this repository, which are written for people who change the code. The iPhone app is held to a copy of the website's words as they stood before this guide was written, which means its words have not been brought up to it yet ([ADR 0037](../adr/0037-the-iphone-app-is-held-to-a-copy-of-the-websites-colours-and-words.md)).

## Who reads the website

The reader is somebody who is choosing where to live, or where to stay. They have never seen Burro before and they have not read its design, which means they do not know what Burro means by a recipe, a band, a release, a measure, a part, a share or "this data". [The design of the website](web.md) pictures them as a person who has never been to London and who arrives from a link.

They are not in a hurry to be impressed. They want to understand what they are looking at, and they want to be able to trust it. Both of those depend on the words, even where a drawing does the talking. A gauge on a result says where an area sits with no sentence beside it, which means that the words for it have to be somewhere a person can find them: the name of the thing stands beside its gauge, the key to the drawings says what each drawing and each mark means, and a screen reader is told in words whatever a drawing says to the eye.

## How to write for them

1. **Write the way a helpful person would explain it aloud.** Use full, natural sentences, the kind you would say to a friend who asked what the page was showing them.
2. **Join your ideas.** Say what follows from what, with the words people use for that: "this means", "because of that", "so", "which is why", "that way", "for example". A sentence that stands alone, with no link to the one before it, reads as a riddle.
3. **Choose being understood over being short.** A longer sentence that a person can follow is better than a short one they have to read twice, so do not cut a sentence down until it is a fragment.
4. **Use everyday words.** Where a word of the product is needed, say what it means the first time it appears on a page. For example: "A vibe is a way of describing what an area feels like."
5. **Say what it means for the reader.** Most people want to know what a figure tells them and not how it was worked out, so lead with the first and keep the second for those who ask.
6. **Use a heading to help somebody find something.** Add one only where a page is long enough to need it. Never add a summary heading, a label over a single sentence, or a heading that repeats the button that opened it.
7. **Let a label be a name.** A label may be short, as "Search", "Compare" and "Refine search" are, but it must still be clear by itself.
8. **Write British English.** Use no exclamation marks and no sales talk, and make no jokes at the reader's expense.

## Say it once, and where it helps

1. **Say a thing once on a page.** Before you add a sentence, read the page from its head to its foot as a visitor does, and ask whether it says the thing already. Deep search said what it is under its tab, and said it again at the head of its settings, because it was explained in each place for itself and the page was not read whole.
2. **Put a sentence where the person who needs it is looking.** A caution that stands on every result is read as doubt, or not read at all. What a little town is, is said where a person asks what the drawings mean, which is the key, and at the head of the page of an area.
3. **Do not pass the workings on.** A visitor wants what Burro found for them. How sure Burro is of its own recipe, which part of a page was built in doubt, and what a test holds are for the people who build Burro.
4. **Do not ask a person what Burro can work out.** Burro applies what it read and shows it, and the person takes off what is wrong. A sentence that asks them to choose is a sentence between them and their answer.
5. **When you take a sentence away, find out what it was there to hold.** Most of the sentences that were taken away were keeping a promise, or had been asked for by a decision record. The record of each says what was given up, and what holds it now. If nothing holds it, say so to the founder before the sentence goes.
6. **Do not write beside a drawing what the drawing says.** Where a gauge shows that an area sits in the middle, words beside it that say "around the middle" are the same thing twice, and the person reading has to work out that they are. Keep those words for somebody who cannot see the drawing, which means that they are said to a screen reader and are not drawn.
7. **Keep words for what a drawing cannot carry.** A drawing cannot give a name, a figure, a source or a date, and it cannot say that something is not known. Those are said in words, in as few as will do, and what is long about them is one press away.
8. **Where you are in doubt between showing more and showing less, show less.** The founder walked the website three times, and each time they kept the pictures and took words away. What you leave out of sight is not thrown away: it stays whole, one press away, for the person who wants it.

One heading is the founder's own, word for word: "Tell me what's important in your space". It is the one place where the website says "me". Everywhere else it says "Burro", and this guide does not ask you to change either.

## "Approx data", and "no data"

One label is the founder's own too. Where a figure on a result is not whole, the website draws a small chequered mark and writes two words beside it: "approx data". The founder asked for those words, and for nothing else beside them.

A result says it of two kinds of figure. The first is where an area sits on a vibe, where Burro could measure only some of the things that go into the vibe for that area. The second is the fit, where it is based on some of the things that count in the search, or where a limit that the person called firm could not be held against the area. Until the third walk a result said each of these in a sentence of its own. It now says "approx data", and every one of those sentences is one press away, under "Show the working".

The website holds the label in one file, `apps/web/src/content/kit.ts`, for a result and for every other page that draws a gauge, so a change to it is made once. The key to the drawings quotes the label, and takes it from there.

A second label was written to go with the first, as the result was built: "no data". It stands beside the name of a thing that the person asked for, where Burro has no figure for it in that area at all. The founder did not give these two words, which means that they are the founder's to change.

| If you are writing | Then |
|---|---|
| The label itself | Write the founder's two words, and do not lengthen them. This guide asks you to choose being understood over being short, but a label is a name, and this one was chosen by the person whose website it is |
| Anything beside the label | Write nothing. How much of a vibe could be measured, and what a fit is based on, belong under "Show the working" and on the page of the area, where they are said in whole sentences as they were |
| What the mark means | It is said once, in the key to the drawings, for the person who asks |
| Something that is not known at all | Do not write "approx data" for it, because that says there is a figure and that it is rough. Write "no data", and never draw the gap as nought or as the middle |
| A journey that Burro estimated from distance | The service says that it is an estimate, in the sentence itself, and the website shows that sentence as it came. Do not add either label to it |
| A sentence of your own that says how sure a figure is | Do not write one. How sure a figure is, is the service's to say, and the website shows its words as they came |

The labels are promises as well as names, because they are how a result keeps the rule that what is not known is said to be not known. That means neither may be left off a line that needs it in order to make a result shorter.

## What does not change

The words change and the promises do not. Each promise below was made before this guide was written, and a sentence that reads beautifully and breaks one of them is the wrong sentence.

| The promise | What it asks of your sentence | Where it is held |
|---|---|---|
| **No verdict on a place** | Nothing is "the best", "a good area", "up and coming", "safe" or "rough". Burro says how well an area matches what the person asked for, and what each figure is. "The closest match" is a fact about the ranking, whereas "the best area" is a verdict | `test_site_copy_never_calls_a_place_safe_or_unsafe` in `apps/web/test/site-copy.test.ts`, and the tests of each page that read it for a verdict |
| **Every figure as the data gives it** | Do not round a figure, soften it, or leave out its unit or its date to make a sentence read better. The website formats no number that the service has already formatted. Every figure has its source and its date, and on a result they are one press away, under "Show the working" | Rule 6 of [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md), and `figuresNotFrom` in the tests of every page that shows figures |
| **What is not known is said to be not known** | It is never filled in, and it is never made to sound like zero or like the middle. Where a figure is rough, a result says "approx data" beside it, and the working says why. Where there is no figure, a result says "no data" | Rule 7 of [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md), and [ADR 0046](../adr/0046-a-result-shows-what-was-asked-for-as-a-name-and-a-gauge-and-the-source-of-every-figure-stands-under-its-working.md) |
| **What the service writes, the website shows as it came** | The names of vibes and of areas, the two ends of a vibe and the sentences of a result all come from the service, so the website changes none of them. If one reads badly, say so, with the words you would put in its place, because the fix belongs in the service | Rule 1 of [apps/web/AGENTS.md](../../apps/web/AGENTS.md) |
| **Made-up data says so** | The banner of every page says it, and it cannot be closed | `test_the_banner_says_the_three_things_it_must` |
| **What is said of privacy stays true** | What is typed, where it goes, and that Burro does not keep it. The website names no company that runs a language model and states none of its terms, because those are the service's to say. Since the founder's second walk it is said on the page of methods, and no longer under the search box | `test_the_line_about_a_persons_words_says_what_burro_does_and_nothing_of_a_provider`, `test_site_copy_names_no_provider_and_states_none_of_its_terms` |
| **Site copy names no place** | The website's own words hold no number and no proper noun about a place, which is why an example sentence names none | `test_site_copy_names_no_place` |
| **A state is said in words** | Colour is never the only sign of anything, so what was assumed, what is chosen and what went wrong are each said in words as well as drawn | [The design of the website](web.md), section 9 |

Some sentences are held more tightly than the rest, because of what they are about. Read the reason before you touch one of them.

| The words | Why they are held | Read first |
|---|---|---|
| When recorded crime counts | It is said one way, in one text that every page that speaks of it repeats word for word, so that no page can promise something another page does not | [ADR 0013](../adr/0013-vibes-are-the-centre.md), and `apps/web/src/content/crime.ts` |
| Anything beside the census figures or the household income of an area | The website says nothing about what such a figure means, and uses no word that would put one group before another | [The design of the website](web.md), section 2.1, with [ADR 0014](../adr/0014-evidence-first-and-census-figures-shown.md) and [ADR 0028](../adr/0028-household-income-is-shown-and-never-ranked-on.md) |
| The sentence of a fact | It gives a figure, what it is a figure of and what it stands against, and no more than that. It may read far better than it did, but it may not judge | `apps/web/src/content/templates.ts` and the test beside it |
| "What counts" in a search | The settings that nobody chose count too, for less, so the website only says "what you asked for" where that is true of the ranking in front of the person | [apps/web/AGENTS.md](../../apps/web/AGENTS.md), under "Rules" |

## Whose words they are

Before you change a sentence, find out whose it is, because that decides where the change is made.

| The words | Are written | To change one |
|---|---|---|
| The names of controls and states, the lines that explain a page, and the pages about the website | In the website, one file for each part, under `apps/web/src/content/` | Change the file, and the test beside it if it held the old words |
| The name and meaning of a vibe, the names of its two ends, the label of a measure, the sentences of a result, a notice, and the message of a failure | By the service, in `packages/core` | Do not reword it in the website. Say which sentence reads badly and what you would put in its place, so that the founder can choose whether the service is changed |
| Who else reads what is typed, and on what terms | By the service, which the search page asks as it opens | The same |

## Where room is short

On the search page the answer comes first: once a ranking follows a press, the first result has to be in sight without scrolling, and on a phone it has to be whole on the first screen. This means that the words which stand between the search box and the first result have very little room, so a longer sentence there can push the answer off the screen.

That is not a reason to write a riddle. Say the thing clearly in the room there is, put what is long one press away, and measure the page in a browser at both sizes before you add a line above the answer. [ADR 0035](../adr/0035-the-search-page-is-one-box-and-the-look-gives-way-to-the-answer.md) says what gives way to the answer, and in which order.

Two smaller things follow from how the website is drawn. The face that sentences are set in has no arrow and no tick, so write neither and let a drawing stand for it. And what a person sees on a short button must be the start of what a screen reader hears, so that somebody who speaks to their device can name the button they see.

## When a test fails on your sentence

Many tests read the words, which is how the promises above are kept. When one fails, work out which of two things has happened.

If the test fails because your sentence reads better than the one it held, rewrite the test so that it holds what it was protecting. For example, a test that held a line word for word to make sure it named no company should go on making sure of that, with the new line.

If the test fails because a promise was broken, the test is right and the sentence is wrong. Change the sentence, and do not weaken the test to let it through.
