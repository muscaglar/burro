# boundaries: Neighbourhood gazetteer and geography model

## Headline
Define about 450 curated, named London neighbourhoods as sets of 2021 Census Output Areas (26,369 in London), seeded only from OGL/CC0/Open Parliament Licence sources (OS Open Names, Wikidata, House of Commons Library MSOA Names, GLA town centres) so the gazetteer stays free of ODbL share-alike. The source of truth is a reviewed text file mapping each OA to a neighbourhood; polygons are generated by dissolve, never hand-drawn. Budget 3 to 4 person-weeks of curation.

## Summary
No usable open polygon set of London neighbourhoods exists, so Burro has to build one. I checked live: OpenStreetMap (data as of 2026-09-22) has 790 named place nodes in Greater London (397 suburb, 115 quarter, 175 neighbourhood, 52 town, 51 village; 589 carry a Wikidata tag) but only 7 suburb and 3 quarter polygons. Overture divisions inherit this from OSM and its docs admit sub-county coverage is "often spotty". Who's On First is a mix of 312 sources. Official postcode polygons are a paid OS product that reaches end of life on 31 March 2028.

Recommended model, three layers:
1. Cell: 2021 Output Area. London has 26,369 OAs, 4,994 LSOAs and 1,002 MSOAs (counted from the ONS lookup service). OAs nest exactly into LSOA, MSOA and borough, follow real features, and come with population-weighted centroids. Average about 330 residents.
2. Neighbourhood: about 450 named areas (range 400 to 500), each a set of OAs. Average roughly 59 OAs, 11 LSOAs, 2.2 MSOAs, about 19,500 residents. A further 250 to 350 smaller or contested names are stored as aliases/sub-areas (points only) that resolve to a parent.
3. Borough (33 including the City) and city above.

Why OA rather than H3 as the base: census counts roll up as exact sums with no areal interpolation; LSOA/MSOA data joins through the official lookup; boundaries look natural rather than jagged. H3 (Apache 2.0; res 9 is 0.105 km2 per cell, about 15,000 cells for London) is kept behind a generic "cell" abstraction for heatmap display and for cities without small census units.

Construction: build a candidate name list from OS Open Names, Wikidata (720 "area of London" items, 717 with coordinates, 703 with an English Wikipedia article) and HoC MSOA Names v2.3 (13 Feb 2026); score salience; assign each OA to the nearest seed by walking-network distance with the Thames as a hard barrier and railways, major roads and borough lines as soft penalties, using MSOA and ward names as priors; then a human reviews and edits the OA assignment.

Licensing posture: everything recommended for v1 permits commercial use with attribution only. OSM is used for QA only and not copied into gazetteer tables, because polygons derived from OSM nodes would plausibly be an ODbL Derivative Database that must be offered to the public.

Two limits on this research: 12 web searches were made, and no files were downloaded for local analysis, so some counts (OS Open Names entries in London, distinct MSOA base names, town centre totals) are unmeasured and listed as unverified.

## Recommendations
- **What is a Burro neighbourhood** [high]: About 450 curated named neighbourhoods (range 400 to 500), each defined as a set of 2021 Output Areas. A second tier of 250 to 350 smaller or contested names (for example Bellenden, Telegraph Hill) is stored as aliases or sub-area points that resolve to a parent neighbourhood for search but are not ranked.
  - why: Source counts bracket the range: Wikidata has 720 areas of London, OSM has 500 suburb/town/village nodes plus 290 quarter/neighbourhood nodes, and there are 1,002 MSOAs. Above roughly 600 units the average falls below two MSOAs and about 15,000 residents, where MSOA-native data stops resolving and rates become unstable. At 450 the average is about 19,500 residents and 59 OAs. Areas with under about 3,000 residents (parks, industrial land, parts of the City) should be flagged not rankable.
  - rejected: MSOAs as neighbourhoods (statistical units with directional names, not how people talk). Wards (679, politically drawn, redrawn in 2022, compound names). Postcode districts (SE15 and N8 each span several perceived neighbourhoods; no open official polygons). Boroughs (far too coarse).
- **Fine-grained unit underneath** [medium]: 2021 Output Area as the canonical cell for membership and statistics, with LSOA 2021 and MSOA 2021 as join levels through the official ONS lookup. Model it as a generic cell table (cell_id, cell_system, geometry, representative point, population) so H3 can be added as a second cell system.
  - why: OAs give exact census roll-up, nest perfectly in the hierarchy, align to roads and rivers, and have population-weighted centroids that sit where people live, which makes them better travel-time origins than hexagon centres that can fall in a park or reservoir. If an all-pairs travel matrix is needed, LSOA centroids (4,994 squared is about 25 million pairs) are tractable and OAs inherit from their LSOA. This needs aligning with the commute workstream.
  - rejected: H3 as the sole base in v1: census data would need dasymetric disaggregation (false precision), and hex unions make jagged boundaries. Res 9 at 200 m edge is too coarse to draw boundaries; res 10 is about 105,000 cells. LSOA as the base: five times coarser than OA, giving blocky neighbourhood edges.
- **Seed sources and licensing posture for the gazetteer** [medium]: Seed names and points only from OS Open Names (OGL), Wikidata (CC0), House of Commons Library MSOA Names (Open Parliament Licence), plus GLA town centre and high street boundaries and ward names (OGL) as salience and anchoring evidence. Use OSM place nodes for QA comparison only and do not copy them into gazetteer tables. Publish one data sources and licences page.
  - why: The gazetteer is the primary key of the whole product and the curated polygons are a core asset. ODbL defines a Derivative Database as any adaptation of a substantial part of the contents, and section 4.6 requires offering it when publicly used. Polygons built by Voronoi around OSM nodes would plausibly qualify. The same names are available under attribution-only licences, so the share-alike exposure is avoidable at no cost. OGL v3 explicitly allows a hyperlink to a page holding the attribution statements.
  - rejected: Seeding directly from OSM place nodes (works technically, but creates a share-alike obligation on the polygon set). Copying Wikipedia list text (CC BY-SA; use Wikidata instead). Any proprietary boundaries from property portals or map providers.
- **How polygons are built** [high]: Seeded partition of OAs. (1) Recentre each seed on its town centre, high street or station rather than trusting a raw coordinate. (2) Assign each OA centroid to the nearest seed by walking-network distance, with the Thames as a hard barrier and railways, trunk roads, large parks and borough lines as soft penalties. (3) Boost assignments where the OA's MSOA name or ward name contains the seed name. (4) Human review edits the assignment. (5) Dissolve OAs to polygons in the build. Source of truth is a CSV of oa21cd to neighbourhood_id (26,369 rows) reviewed by pull-request diff.
  - why: Reproducible, diffable and friendly to both agents and humans. An agent can propose reassignments and a person approves them. Boundaries inherit sensible edges from OA geometry. No one ever edits vertices.
  - rejected: Hand-drawing polygons (slow, unreproducible, cannot be diffed). Plain Euclidean Voronoi (ignores river and rail severance). Merging MSOAs by base name only (fast but coarse; keep it as a prior and as a two-day v0 for internal demos).
- **Fuzzy and overlapping boundaries** [medium]: Keep a crisp partition for ranking (each OA has one primary neighbourhood) but give the membership table a weight column defaulting to 1.0 so secondary memberships can be added later without a migration. Render soft, unlabelled edges on the map, call areas approximate, and add a report-this-boundary link. Store overlaps-with relations between neighbourhoods.
  - why: Deterministic, explainable ranking needs each resident counted once. Most user distrust comes from hard lines on the map rather than from the underlying statistics, so the fuzziness belongs in presentation first.
  - rejected: Fully fuzzy kernel membership in v1 (harder to explain and test). Overlapping polygons (double counting, ambiguous links).
- **Rolling statistics up to neighbourhoods** [high]: Store every metric at cell level with a declared aggregation: sum for counts, population-weighted mean or median for rates, indices and travel times, area-weighted for land cover. For LSOA or MSOA native data, apply the parent value to each child OA for rates, or apportion by OA population share for counts. Record native_resolution per metric and expose it to the explanation layer. Weights come from Census 2021 OA populations, refreshed from ONS small-area estimates.
  - why: Because neighbourhoods are unions of OAs, areal interpolation is only needed when a source is not on census geography. Surfacing native resolution lets the LLM say truthfully that a figure is estimated from a wider area.
  - rejected: Pure area-weighted interpolation (badly wrong where parks or industrial land dominate). Hiding source resolution (creates false precision).
- **Parks, water and empty land** [medium]: Use ONS boundaries clipped to mean high water (BGC for display, BFC for spatial joins). Let large parks fall into whichever neighbourhood their OA belongs to; population weighting makes them neutral for rates. Compute density on inhabited area (OA area minus OS Open Greenspace and water). Optionally cut the largest parks and reservoirs out of display polygons.
  - why: Population weighting solves the statistical problem; the rest is cartography. Gross-area densities would make Richmond or Hampstead look misleadingly empty.
  - rejected: Treating large parks as their own neighbourhoods (no residents, breaks ranking).
- **Hierarchy** [high]: City, then optional sub-region, then borough (33), then neighbourhood (about 450), then sub-area or alias (points). Neighbourhood to borough is many-to-many with a population share and one primary borough.
  - why: Many real neighbourhoods straddle borough lines (Crystal Palace, Finsbury Park, Kilburn). A strict tree would force wrong answers for council tax and schools context.
  - rejected: Strict nesting inside boroughs.
- **Stable IDs, slugs and versioning** [high]: Immutable opaque ID per neighbourhood (for example nbh_ plus 8 base32 characters), never reused. Human slug unique within a city (/london/peckham), with borough suffix on collisions (hayes-hillingdon, hayes-bromley). A slug_history table drives redirects. Shared links and saved shortlists store IDs plus a gazetteer_version. Splits and merges set superseded_by. Keep cross-references to Wikidata QID, OS Open Names ID and constituent GSS codes.
  - why: Boundaries will be edited after launch. IDs must survive that, and an old shared link should still explain what the user saw.
  - rejected: Using Wikidata QIDs, OSM node IDs or Overture GERS IDs as primary keys (external lifecycle; GERS changed format in June 2025). Slugs as keys (they change).
- **Neighbours and nearby similar areas** [medium]: Adjacency graph derived from shared OA borders, with edge attributes for shared border length and walking and transit minutes between centres; river crossings flagged. Nearby means within k hops or a travel-time threshold. Similar means distance over the same standardised feature vector used for ranking, precomputed top 20 per neighbourhood, returned with the top contributing features.
  - why: Both are deterministic and explainable, and reuse data already computed. It supports prompts like similar to Peckham but cheaper.
  - rejected: LLM-judged similarity (not deterministic, invites invented facts). Pure centroid distance (ignores the river and rail severance).
- **Extending to other cities** [medium]: Keep the engine dependent only on: city, cells with population and a representative point, neighbourhoods, a cell to neighbourhood weight table, and a metric catalogue. England and Wales cities reuse the identical pipeline because every recommended source is national. Elsewhere, use H3 res 9 cells with official neighbourhood polygons where they exist, or Overture divisions.
  - why: Manchester or Bristol become a curation task rather than an engineering task. H3 earns its place for cities without small census units.
  - rejected: Hard-coding OA concepts throughout the scoring engine.
- **Curation effort and process** [medium]: Plan 100 to 150 hours (3 to 4 person-weeks) for a launch-quality London gazetteer: name reconciliation and tiering 2 to 3 days; boundary review at 5 to 10 minutes per neighbourhood, 7 to 8 days; a borough-by-borough sanity pass with local knowledge, about 4 days; tooling (QGIS project or a small review page plus scripts) 2 to 3 days. Then 1 to 2 days per quarter. A rough internal v0 is possible in 2 to 3 days.
  - why: Agents can draft matches and propose assignments, but roughly two thirds of the time is judgement about where places begin and end, which needs someone who knows London. Underestimating this is the most likely way the product feels wrong on day one.
  - rejected: Fully automated boundaries with no review. Buying commercial neighbourhood polygons (over budget, and licence terms typically restrict redistribution).

## Risks
- [high] Users dispute boundaries (that is not Peckham), undermining trust in the rankings. -> Soft edges on the map, approximate wording, a report-this-boundary link, a second reviewer with local knowledge, and a versioned gazetteer so fixes ship without breaking links.
- [high] Curation effort is underestimated and the gazetteer ships rough. -> Ring-fence 3 to 4 person-weeks. Do inner London and the most searched areas first. Ship a two to three day v0 internally to unblock engineering while curation continues.
- [medium] ODbL share-alike attaches to the polygon set if OSM data is mixed into the gazetteer. -> Seed only from OGL, CC0 and Open Parliament Licence sources. Keep OSM in separate tables. Maintain a per-dataset licence manifest in the repo. Get a short legal read before launch.
- [medium] Rankings are sensitive to where boundaries fall (modifiable areal unit problem). -> Compute at cell level, use population-weighted medians, show within-neighbourhood spread, and run a sensitivity test that reassigns border OAs.
- [medium] False precision when borough or MSOA level data is shown per neighbourhood. -> Record native_resolution per metric and have the explanation layer state when a figure comes from a wider area.
- [medium] OS Open Names may not cover enough London neighbourhood names. -> Wikidata (720 items) and MSOA Names are independent fallbacks. Measure coverage in the first day of the build.
- [medium] Small-population areas (the City, industrial land, large parks) produce unstable rates. -> Minimum resident threshold of about 3,000 for ranked neighbourhoods; flag others as not rankable but still viewable.
- [low] Census geography is redrawn after 2031, or ONS revises lookups. -> Cell system is versioned (oa21). ONS publishes old-to-new best-fit lookups; rebuild the assignment from those.
- [low] Slug collisions and renames break shared links. -> Links carry immutable IDs. Slugs are disambiguated by borough and redirected through slug_history.
- [low] Seed coordinates from Wikidata or OS sit in the wrong place, skewing the automatic partition. -> Recentre seeds on town centre or high street polygons and stations before partitioning.


## Open questions
- Is the founder content to keep the gazetteer free of OSM-derived data (my recommendation), or is publishing the neighbourhood polygons under ODbL acceptable? The latter would allow direct use of OSM nodes.
- Who curates? Does the founder have the London knowledge, and is there budget for a second reviewer?
- Should small but famous Zone 1 areas (Soho, Fitzrovia, Barbican) be ranked neighbourhoods despite low resident populations?
- Is v1 strictly Greater London, or should commuter towns just outside (Watford, Epsom, Dartford) be included for relocators? The data model supports either.
- Will the commute workstream compute travel times from OA centroids (26,369), LSOA centroids (4,994) or a hex grid? This needs agreeing so both use the same cell table.
- Crisp membership only in v1, or secondary memberships for border OAs from the start?
- How many distinct base names do London's 1,002 MSOA names collapse to, and how many OS Open Names populated places fall inside Greater London? Both are quick measurements for the first day of the build.
