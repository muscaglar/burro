# housing-cost: Housing cost data (rent and sale price levels by small area, London)

## Headline
Build the v1 housing-cost layer entirely on Open Government Licence data. Sale prices: aggregate HM Land Registry Price Paid Data yourself to Burro neighbourhoods by property type (36-month window, indexed to today with UK HPI). Rents: anchor on ONS Price Index of Private Rents (borough x bedroom, monthly, official statistics) and distribute to neighbourhoods using a partial-pooling model that blends (a) ONS 'Private rental market in London' postcode-district x bedroom medians and (b) Land Registry flat-price relativities, published as labelled ranges with confidence tiers. Add PropertyData API (GBP 28-48/month) only as a non-critical calibration layer for new-let asking rents. Reject Rightmove, Zoopla/Hometrack and OnTheMarket for v1, and do not deep-link to the big portals without written consent because their terms forbid it. The address of each company's page is under 'Pages of named companies' below.

## Summary
Verified on 2026-09-23 against primary sources. Of a company's page, this report says that it was read and on what day, and no more. A person opens each in a browser before anything rests on it.

SALE PRICES are a solved problem with open data. Price Paid Data (OGL v3, monthly, latest July 2026, 5.3 GB full CSV) gives every registered sale with postcode, price, date, property type (D/S/T/F/O), new-build flag and tenure. It has no bedroom count and no floor area. ONS publishes ready-made MSOA medians by property type (latest 17 Sep 2026, year ending March 2026, now apparently twice yearly) which are good for validation but do not match Burro's neighbourhood polygons. The London Datastore equivalents are stale (to Dec 2017) and should be ignored.

RENTS are the gap, but it is smaller than expected. There is no official rent statistic below local authority level in the regular releases. However ONS publishes a quarterly ad hoc workbook, 'Private rental market in London', under OGL with count, mean, lower quartile, median and upper quartile by POSTCODE DISTRICT x bedroom category (Room, Studio, 1, 2, 3, 4+). The latest edition was opened once for this research (April 2025 to March 2026, published 27 April 2026, at <https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/inflationandpriceindices/adhocs/3389privaterentalmarketinlondonapril2025tomarch2026>). No copy of it is in the repository, and the name of its sheet was not written down. It holds 329 district codes, 247 with at least one published median, 1-bed medians for 231 and 2-bed for 235. No figure for a district is given here. W1 sub-districts and most City (EC2/EC3/EC4) districts are entirely unavailable. ONS attaches strong caveats: purposive sample, not quality-adjusted, 'should not be compared across time periods or between areas', and not comparable with PIPR. The edition due July 2026 was not on the ONS listing as of today, and the GLA London Rents Map has switched from this series to borough-level PIPR, so continuity is a real risk.

The credible approach is therefore a model, not a lookup: PIPR borough x bedroom level (official, monthly, stock of achieved rents) multiplied by a neighbourhood relativity that is a precision-weighted blend of the observed district/borough rent ratio and a damped flat-price ratio from Price Paid Data. There is a direct public-sector precedent: the GLA's London Living Rent method (January 2026 note) disaggregates borough benchmarks to wards using the ratio of ward to borough median flat prices, rescaled to a maximum of plus or minus 20 per cent.

HONESTY ISSUE: PIPR measures the whole stock of tenancies (new and existing), so it understates what someone moving in today will be asked to pay. This matters most for the relocator persona. PropertyData's /rents endpoint (asking rents from live portal listings, by postcode district or sector and bedroom count, 1 credit per call) can calibrate a separate 'moving in now' figure for about GBP 28-48/month (<https://propertydata.co.uk/api/pricing>), and its terms (updated August 2026, <https://propertydata.co.uk/terms>) permit use inside customer-facing products on every plan, with a 60-day cache limit.

COMMERCIAL PORTALS: Rightmove's data page gives no price and points to its sales team (<https://www.rightmove.co.uk/data/>). Zoopla's terms ban scraping, text and data mining and AI use (<https://www.zoopla.co.uk/terms/>). Hometrack's data page gives no price (<https://www.hometrack.com/data-services/data-hub/>). No data product or developer programme was found on OnTheMarket's terms page (<https://www.onthemarket.com/terms/>). No public API was found on any page read. Whether one exists was not read.

LINKING OUT: Rightmove's terms of use (last updated February 2026, section 13, <https://www.rightmove.co.uk/c/terms-of-use/>) prohibit linking directly to any page 'for the purpose of commercial gain' and prohibit including links in any other website or application without prior written consent from its legal team. Zoopla's terms forbid links to anything other than the home page without prior agreement (<https://www.zoopla.co.uk/terms/>). OnTheMarket's terms forbid deep links (<https://www.onthemarket.com/terms/>). So portal deep links and any affiliate revenue from them require a negotiated agreement; treat as post-launch business development, not a v1 feature.

PRICE PER SQUARE METRE: defer. EPC non-address fields including UPRN are OGL, but EPC address fields are restricted to eight energy-related purposes, so address-matching EPC to Price Paid for a commercial product is not clean. HM Land Registry began publishing a Price Paid to UPRN lookup table (OGL) on 28 August 2026, which makes a clean UPRN join possible going forward, but it is not backfilled.

## Recommendations
- **Source of truth for sale price levels** [high]: HM Land Registry Price Paid Data, aggregated in-house to Burro neighbourhoods by property type (flat, terraced, semi, detached). Use a rolling 36-month window, index each sale to the current month using UK HPI borough x property-type series, exclude Category B (additional) transactions and type 'O', publish median plus lower and upper quartile, and require a minimum sample (suggest n>=30) before showing a type-specific figure; otherwise pool with adjacent neighbourhoods and lower the confidence tier.
  - why: OGL, commercial use explicit, postcode-level so it rolls up to any polygon, monthly refresh, 30 years of history. Computing it ourselves gives control over windows, exclusions and geography. ONS MSOA medians (latest 17 Sep 2026) become a free regression test for the pipeline.
  - rejected: ONS MSOA/LSOA median datasets as primary (MSOA does not match named neighbourhoods, now twice-yearly; LSOA series last released Sept 2023). London Datastore 'Average House Prices by Borough, Ward, MSOA & LSOA' (stale, ends Dec 2017). Commercial services priced per property (the wrong shape and cost for area aggregates).
- **Source of truth for rent levels** [high]: ONS Price Index of Private Rents (PIPR) borough x bedroom monthly rent levels as the anchor for every neighbourhood estimate.
  - why: Official statistics (status since 20 May 2026), OGL, monthly with about 3-4 weeks lag (August 2026 data published 16 Sep 2026), based on roughly 450,000 achieved rents a year in England, quality-adjusted via hedonic model, broken down by bedroom count. Anchoring to it means Burro's borough totals always reconcile with a citable official number.
  - rejected: ONS Private Rental Market Summary Statistics (discontinued, last edition Dec 2023). London Datastore 'Average Private Rents, Borough' (no longer updated). LHA rates (30th percentile only, 14 or so broad areas, frozen at 2024-25 levels). Scraping portals (prohibited by terms and by the brief).
- **How to fill the small-area rent gap** [medium]: Partial-pooling model. rent[n,b] = PIPR[borough(n), b] x R[n]. R[n] = exp( w x log(r_obs) + (1-w) x beta x log(p) ), where r_obs is the district-to-borough median rent ratio taken from the SAME ONS London ad hoc workbook (so sample differences cancel), p is the neighbourhood-to-borough median flat price ratio from Price Paid Data (5-year window, HPI-adjusted, flats only to limit mix effects), beta is an elasticity calibrated on districts with count >= 100 (expect below 1 because rents vary less than prices), and w = n/(n+k) with k around 50. Clamp R to a sane band (suggest 0.7 to 1.4). Map postcode districts to neighbourhoods by household-weighted overlap using Census 2021 output areas. Backtest with leave-one-out on high-count districts and publish the error on a public methodology page.
  - why: Uses the only open small-area rent observations that exist, respects ONS's warning that the raw district figures are not robust for area comparison by shrinking low-count districts toward a price-based prior, degrades gracefully if ONS stops publishing the ad hoc series (w goes to zero), and mirrors a published GLA method (London Living Rent disaggregates borough to ward by median flat price ratio, capped at plus or minus 20 per cent).
  - rejected: Showing raw ONS postcode-district medians as-is (many cells have n of 10-40, ONS says do not compare areas, W1 and City are blank). Borough rent x raw price ratio with no damping (overstates rent gradients; prime areas have low yields). Buying a modelled rent surface from a commercial supplier (no published method, which fails the explainability goal).
- **Paid data in v1** [medium]: PropertyData API on the GBP 28/month (2,000 credits) or GBP 48/month (5,000 credits) plan (<https://propertydata.co.uk/api/pricing>), used only to (1) estimate a borough or district level new-let asking-rent uplift shown as a separate 'moving in now' figure and (2) validate the open-data model. Refresh monthly; about 250 districts x 4 bedroom bands is about 1,000 calls. Keep it behind an interface so the product works without it.
  - why: Fits the stated budget, self-serve, and the terms (last updated August 2026, <https://propertydata.co.uk/terms>) state that using the API inside a customer-facing product including paid multi-tenant SaaS is permitted on every plan with no separate licence. It fixes the biggest honesty gap: PIPR is a stock measure and understates rents for someone signing a new tenancy. Storing derived calibration coefficients is within the terms; raw responses must be refreshed or deleted after 60 days.
  - rejected: Rightmove Data Services and Zoopla/Hometrack (no price on the pages read: <https://www.rightmove.co.uk/data/>, <https://www.hometrack.com/data-services/data-hub/>). Making PropertyData the displayed source of truth (creates hard dependency, 60-day cache rule, must delete cached data on account closure, upstream provenance from portal listings is outside Burro's control).
- **Bedrooms versus property type** [high]: Renters filter by bedroom count (Room, Studio, 1, 2, 3, 4+), which both PIPR and the ONS London workbook support. Buyers filter by property type in v1 because Price Paid Data has no bedroom field. Use VOA Council Tax Stock of Properties and Census 2021 (TS044, TS050, TS054) to describe the housing mix of each neighbourhood so users can see, for example, that an area is 80 per cent flats.
  - why: Avoids inventing bedroom-level sale prices that the data cannot support. Matches how each data source is actually cut.
  - rejected: Estimating price by bedroom count from EPC habitable rooms in v1 (requires EPC to Price Paid linkage, which is not licence-clean for historic data).
- **Price per square metre** [medium]: Defer to after v1. From now on, accumulate the HM Land Registry transaction-to-UPRN lookup (published monthly since 28 August 2026, OGL) and join to EPC total floor area on UPRN, never on address. Seek written clarification from the UCL authors before using their linked 1995 to October 2024 dataset for backfill.
  - why: EPC address fields are licensed only for eight energy-related purposes; commercial address-matching is outside them. UPRN is OGL in both datasets, so a UPRN join is clean, but the lookup is not backfilled so coverage will take a year or more to become useful. The UCL dataset shows CC BY 4.0 on UK Data Service ReShare but the London Datastore copy says it is published for non-commercial purposes.
  - rejected: Address-matching EPC to Price Paid ourselves (licence risk on EPC address data). Buying AddressBase (cost and licensing overhead disproportionate for v1).
- **Affordability metrics** [medium]: Renters: (1) budget fit expressed in words from the estimated distribution, for example 'about half of 2-beds here are within your budget'; (2) rent as a share of gross household income against the ONS 30 per cent threshold, only when the user gives income; (3) per-person cost for sharers (N-bed rent divided by N) and the Room category where available; (4) rent plus borough council tax as monthly housing cost. Buyers: (1) budget fit by property type; (2) deposit needed at 10 per cent and indicative income needed; (3) price-to-earnings context against the ONS five-times-earnings threshold; (4) price per square metre later.
  - why: Uses the same definitions ONS publishes (30 per cent of gross income for rent; five years of earnings for purchase), so every threshold is citable. Budget fit is the metric users actually act on and it is deterministic.
  - rejected: A single opaque 'affordability score' (not explainable). Mortgage payment calculators with live rates in v1 (regulatory and freshness burden).
- **How to present estimates honestly** [high]: Every figure carries: the word 'estimated', a range not a point (lower to upper quartile), rounding to the nearest GBP 25 for rent and GBP 5,000 for price, an 'as of' month, a confidence tier (High: observed n>=50; Medium: n 10-49 blended; Low: modelled from prices only), and a source line. Keep 'typical rent paid' (PIPR-anchored) visibly separate from 'asking rent if you move in now'. Expose a public methodology page with backtest error. The LLM may only quote numbers present in the structured payload and must repeat the tier wording.
  - why: OGL forbids implying official endorsement and requires attribution; ONS explicitly warns about the district data; false precision is the main reputational risk for a data-led product.
  - rejected: Single headline number per area. Hiding low-confidence areas entirely (leaves holes in central London, where W1 and City have no district data).
- **Linking out to listing portals** [high]: No deep links to Rightmove, Zoopla or OnTheMarket in v1. Build an 'outbound provider' abstraction that turns a neighbourhood plus filters into a link, ship it disabled, and open partnership conversations once there is traffic. If an outbound link is wanted at launch, restrict to portals whose terms do not prohibit linking, and get written confirmation first.
  - why: Rightmove terms section 13.1 and 13.4 (<https://www.rightmove.co.uk/c/terms-of-use/>), Zoopla terms clause 6 (<https://www.zoopla.co.uk/terms/>) and OnTheMarket terms clause 3.4 (<https://www.onthemarket.com/terms/>) each prohibit deep links without consent. Breaching them early would damage later partnership prospects.
  - rejected: Building a link to a portal's search page (the terms above prohibit such a link). Embedding listing cards or images (the same terms).
- **Refresh cadence** [high]: One monthly batch run, scheduled a few days after the later of the ONS PIPR release (second or third Wednesday) and the Price Paid release (20th working day). Quarterly check for a new ONS London ad hoc workbook. Version every derived table with source edition and as-of date so shared links and saved shortlists can show what changed.
  - why: All primary sources are monthly or slower; there is no benefit in anything more frequent, and versioning supports explainability.
  - rejected: Live API calls at query time (slower, costlier, non-deterministic rankings).

## Risks
- [high] ONS stops publishing the London postcode-district rent workbook. The edition due July 2026 was not listed on 2026-09-23 and the GLA Rents Map no longer depends on it. -> Design the rent model so the observed-rent weight can fall to zero and the price-ratio prior carries the estimate. Archive every past edition now (ten editions since Feb 2024 are listed). Ask ONS, at hpi@ons.gov.uk, about its plans, and if needed submit Burro's own ad hoc request. Keep PropertyData as a paid fallback signal.
- [high] Users treat PIPR-anchored figures as the rent they will pay, but PIPR measures the whole stock including long-standing tenancies, so new-let asking rents are typically higher. -> Show two clearly labelled figures: 'typical rent paid' and 'asking rent if you move in now' (calibrated from asking-rent data). State the difference in the explanation text and methodology page.
- [medium] Small samples make neighbourhood figures noisy: median postcode-district count is about 70 for 2-beds and about 10 for rooms, studios and 4+ beds; houses are rare in inner London and flats rare in parts of outer London. -> Partial pooling, minimum-sample thresholds, confidence tiers, ranges instead of points, and pooling with adjacent neighbourhoods. Suppress a property type where the stock share is negligible.
- [medium] Misuse of address data. Price Paid address fields and EPC address fields carry Royal Mail and Ordnance Survey restrictions, and EPC address-level data is personal data. -> Never display or export address-level records. Drop address columns at ingestion, keeping postcode only for aggregation and UPRN for joins. Do not address-match EPC data. Record this rule in the repo's data conventions.
- [medium] Breaching portal terms by deep-linking harms later partnership prospects or triggers a takedown demand. -> No deep links to Rightmove, Zoopla or OnTheMarket without written consent. Keep any paid source as calibration only so it can be removed without changing the product.
- [medium] PropertyData changes its terms or its prices; stored answers must be refreshed or deleted after 60 days (<https://propertydata.co.uk/terms>). -> Store only derived calibration factors with dates, schedule the monthly refresh, and isolate it behind a provider interface.
- [medium] Price Paid distortions: shared-ownership part sales, bulk or corporate transactions, new-build premiums and leasehold short-lease discounts skew medians in regeneration areas. -> Exclude Category B and type 'Other', report new-build share, use medians and quartiles rather than means, and flag neighbourhoods where new-build share exceeds a threshold.
- [low] Data lag in a moving market. Price Paid is effectively about three months behind; PIPR about one month; the district rent workbook is a 12-month average published a month after period end. -> Index to the current month using UK HPI and PIPR, and always show the as-of month.
- [medium] The LLM states a price or rent that is not in the data. -> Pass only structured facts with provenance to the model, require it to quote figures verbatim with tier wording, and add an automated check that every number in generated text exists in the payload.
- [low] Licence ambiguity on the UCL price-per-square-metre dataset and on GLA London Living Rent files. -> Do not ship either in v1; seek written confirmation before use.


## Open questions
- Should room and flatshare rents be a first-class option for renters? Many relocators share, and the ONS London workbook has a Room category but with thin coverage (80 of 329 districts).
- Will ONS continue the quarterly 'Private rental market in London' postcode-district release now that the GLA map uses PIPR? A question for ONS, at hpi@ons.gov.uk.
- May blended estimates derived partly from PropertyData's API appear on public, unauthenticated area pages?
- For buyers, is filtering by property type (flat, terraced, semi, detached) acceptable for v1, given that Land Registry data has no bedroom count?
- Should shared ownership and new-build-only buyers be treated as separate cases?
- Does the founder want outbound listing links at launch at all, given they need written consent from the large portals, or is 'area discovery only' acceptable for v1?
- Which budget tier for PropertyData: district level (about 1,000 calls a month, GBP 28) or sector level (roughly 4,500 calls, GBP 48)? Prices as read at <https://propertydata.co.uk/api/pricing>.
- Is the UCL linked price-per-square-metre dataset CC BY 4.0 for commercial use, as the UK Data Service record says, or non-commercial as the London Datastore page says?
- Should council tax by borough be included in the monthly housing cost figure? The source was not checked in this pass.
