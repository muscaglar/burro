# liveability-demographics: Liveability and demographic statistics (London, small-area)

## Headline
Build v1 on a 34-feature, 9-dimension store computed at LSOA 2021 (4,994 London LSOAs) and rolled up to named neighbourhoods, using only sources whose open licence I confirmed today: data.police.uk, Indices of Deprivation 2025 (File 8 underlying indicators is a big shortcut), Census 2021 via Nomis, ONS population estimates, GIAS + Ofsted MI + DfE performance tables, NHS ODS, Defra noise Round 4 and UK-AIR PCM, EA NaFRA2 flood data, OS Open Greenspace, London Public Realm Trees, Ofcom Connected Nations bulk files, DfT traffic and road safety, and GLA Cultural Infrastructure Map. Score with within-London percentile ranks, user-weighted, with missing features dropped and weights renormalised. Keep every protected-characteristic census table out of the ranking feature store entirely. Hold LAEI 2022 and Planning London Datahub until GLA confirms the licence in writing, and reject GiGL, the Ofcom API, Curio Canopy and the Westminster LTN dataset.

## Summary
Almost everything needed is free and under OGL v3, so the data budget for this domain is zero pounds. The work is in engineering and judgement, not procurement.

Key facts confirmed today:
- Indices of Deprivation 2025 WAS released (30 Oct 2025, updated 17 Nov 2025), on 2021 LSOAs (33,755 in England), OGL v3. 55 indicators versus 39 in 2019: 20 new, 14 significantly modified, 3 removed. Domain weights unchanged (Income 22.5, Employment 22.5, Education 13.5, Health 13.5, Crime 9.3, Barriers 9.3, Living Environment 9.3). Income is now measured after housing costs, which pushed London's income deprivation ranks up sharply. New LSOA-level indicators directly useful to Burro: noise (share of residents exposed to 55 dB Lden or more from road, rail AND aircraft), broadband speed, patient-to-GP ratio, housing lacking private outdoor space, housing energy performance, anti-social behaviour, connectivity score. File 8 publishes these underlying indicators. Not comparable with 2019.
- Ofsted: overall effectiveness grades stopped in September 2024. Inspections Sept 2024 to Nov 2025 show 'Not judged' overall with key judgements only. From 10 Nov 2025 report cards grade 6 core evaluation areas (plus early years and sixth form where relevant) on a 5-point scale (Exceptional, Strong standard, Expected standard, Needs attention, Urgent improvement), safeguarding met/not met, and NO composite grade. Only 937 inspections (about 4% of 21,965 schools) had been done under it by 31 March 2026. Ofsted says the frameworks are not directly comparable. The monthly management information CSV (about 16 MB) mixes all three regimes.
- Progress 8 cannot be calculated for 2024/25 or 2025/26 (no KS2 baseline), so secondary comparisons must use Attainment 8 and grade 5+ English and maths.
- There is no national school catchment dataset. Do not claim catchments in v1.
- GiGL withdrew 'Spaces to Visit' as open data in October 2025. Use OS Open Greenspace instead.
- Ofcom bulk files are OGL, but mobile coverage is only published at local authority level; the Ofcom API forbids building a dataset or caching beyond one month, so it is incompatible with precomputation.
- TfL Cycling Infrastructure Database files were last modified September 2021, so it is a stale baseline.
- LAEI 2022 (20 m NO2/PM2.5 grid, revised May 2026) shows no licence on the London Datastore and 'Not set' on data.gov.uk. Same for Planning London Datahub.

Scoring approach: compute raw values per LSOA (or per OA/H3 cell for point data), apply empirical-Bayes shrinkage to small-count rates, convert to within-London percentile ranks with declared polarity, aggregate to neighbourhoods by population weighting (people-facing features) or area weighting (environmental features), then combine with user weights as a weighted mean of percentiles. Explanations are per-feature contributions plus the raw value and its source and vintage.

Protected characteristics: ethnicity, religion, country of birth, national identity, language, sexual orientation, gender identity, disability and health, sex, and marital status must never be rankable or filterable, and the LLM must only be able to emit feature IDs from an allowlisted registry. Age and life-stage variables are descriptive only. User prompts that reveal religion, sexuality or health become special category data under ICO guidance if Burro infers from or acts on them, so prompts should not be persisted against accounts by default.

## Recommendations
- **Base computation geography** [high]: Compute at LSOA 2021 for published statistics and at OA 2021 or H3 resolution 9 for point and raster data, then roll up to named neighbourhoods. Use population-weighted aggregation for people-facing features (crime, schools, GP, demographics) and area-weighted for environmental ones (air, noise, flood, green cover).
  - why: IoD 2025, ONS population estimates, data.police.uk and Census 2021 are all natively on 2021 LSOAs or OAs. London has 4,994 LSOAs, small enough to precompute everything in minutes. Keeping the fine layer lets neighbourhood boundaries be redrawn without recomputing sources.
  - rejected: Wards (boundaries change, too coarse, few datasets publish on them). MSOA (about 1,000 in London, too coarse for a few hundred neighbourhoods). Postcode sectors (not statistical units, Royal Mail IP issues).
- **v1 feature set (34 features, 9 dimensions)** [medium]: SAFETY: violent crime and robbery rate; burglary and theft rate; anti-social behaviour rate; pedestrian and cyclist casualty rate. SCHOOLS: count of primaries within 1 km walk; nearby primary KS2 expected-standard share; nearby secondary Attainment 8; share of nearby schools with a current adverse Ofsted outcome. HEALTH AND SERVICES: patient-to-GP ratio; walk time to nearest GP surgery; distance to nearest hospital with A and E. GREEN AND BLUE: public greenspace share of area; walk distance to nearest park of 2 ha or more; street and public tree density; private outdoor space; distance to water. AIR, NOISE, FLOOD: NO2 annual mean; PM2.5 annual mean; share of residents at 55 dB Lden or more; share of residential area at high or medium river and sea flood risk; same for surface water. DIGITAL: gigabit availability share of premises; average download speed. STREETS: protected cycle track length per km of road; cycle parking density; major-road traffic exposure. BUZZ AND CULTURE: pubs and bars density; restaurants and cafes density; cultural venue density (music, theatre, cinema, gallery, library); night venue density. HOMES AND PEOPLE (descriptive, positive-preference only): population density; private renting share; flats share; households with dependent children share; student share; residential churn. CHANGE: new homes completed or approved per 1,000 dwellings (only once licence confirmed; otherwise ship 33).
  - why: Every feature has a confirmed-open source, a clear polarity a user can understand, and London-wide coverage. Dimensions map to words users actually say (safe, green, quiet, lively, good schools).
  - rejected: A single 'liveability index' (not explainable, hides trade-offs). Using the IMD rank as a ranking feature (it is a deprivation measure of residents and a proxy for protected characteristics; show as context only).
- **What to defer beyond v1** [high]: Defer: mobile coverage (bulk data is local-authority only); CQC ratings and GP Patient Survey; LAEI 2022 20 m air quality and Planning London Datahub (pending licence confirmation); school admissions cut-off distances; LTN boundaries; tree canopy cover; premises-level alcohol licensing; live air quality sensor readings; TfL CID as a primary source; geodemographic classifications (LOAC).
  - why: Each is either too coarse, stale, unlicensed, or needs manual collection across 33 boroughs.
  - rejected: Scraping borough licensing registers or admissions brochures (no consistent licence, high maintenance).
- **Normalisation for weighted scoring** [high]: 1) Convert counts to rates with a sensible denominator (crime: residents plus workday population; venues: per km2 or distance-decay accessibility, not raw in-polygon counts). 2) Apply empirical-Bayes shrinkage toward the borough mean for small-count rates, as IoD does. 3) Convert to within-London percentile rank using mid-rank for ties, scaled 0 to 100, flipped so higher is always better for the stated preference. 4) Combine as a weighted arithmetic mean of percentiles within each dimension, then across dimensions with user weights. 5) Keep raw values and units alongside for explanations.
  - why: Percentile ranks are immune to skew and outliers (the City of London and Westminster crime rates would wreck min-max or z-scores), are easy to explain ('quieter than 80% of London'), and make features commensurable. Shrinkage stops tiny LSOAs producing extreme rates.
  - rejected: Z-scores (assume symmetry, meaningless to users). Min-max (dominated by outliers). IoD-style exponential transform (designed to emphasise deprivation tails, not needed here). National percentiles (London clusters at one end for density, air and transport, so features lose discriminating power).
- **Missing and zero-inflated data** [high]: Never impute silently. If a feature is missing for an area, drop it from that area's score, renormalise the remaining weights, and show a coverage badge. Refuse to score a dimension with under half its weight present. For zero-inflated venue counts use distance-decay accessibility (for example sum of exp(-d/500 m)) so neighbouring high streets count and ties are rare. Store vintage per feature and show it.
  - why: Median imputation makes unknown areas look average and is indefensible in an explainable ranker. Boundary effects are severe for named neighbourhoods whose high street sits on the edge.
  - rejected: Median or KNN imputation in the ranking path. Dropping areas with any missing feature.
- **Treatment of protected characteristics** [medium]: Hard-exclude from the ranking feature store: ethnicity, religion, country of birth, national identity, main language, sexual orientation, gender identity, disability, general health, sex, legal partnership status. Enforce with a code-level allowlist of feature IDs that the LLM must choose from. Age and life-stage (households with children, students) are shown descriptively and may be used only as a positive soft preference, never to exclude. Map people-based vibe requests to place-based features (venues, housing type, amenities). Let users search for amenities (places of worship, LGBTQ+ venues, specialist shops) as proximity features. Do not persist raw prompts against accounts by default; store only the structured preferences.
  - why: The nine Equality Act 2010 protected characteristics are confirmed on legislation.gov.uk. ICO guidance confirms that inferring or acting on special categories is special category processing regardless of confidence. Redfin said in December 2021 that it would not show neighbourhood crime data, citing the risk of reinforcing racial bias (<https://www.redfin.com/news/neighborhood-crime-data-doesnt-belong-on-real-estate-sites/>), which shows the reputational stakes even for non-protected proxies.
  - rejected: Showing ethnicity or religion breakdowns on profile pages 'for information' (invites steering claims and press risk for little product value). Letting the LLM free-form interpret requests about who lives somewhere.
- **Crime presentation** [medium]: Use data.police.uk bulk CSVs, 24-month rolling window, by category, as rates with residents-plus-workday denominators and shrinkage. Show categories separately with plain caveats. Do not publish a single headline 'safety score' on profile pages; allow safety as a user-weighted dimension only.
  - why: Locations are snapped to about 679,000 anonymised points, so town centres and stations attract counts; location accuracy varies by force (60 to 97% per data.police.uk). Category-level rates are more honest than one number.
  - rejected: MPS London Datastore file as primary (omits sexual offences at LSOA, excludes City of London Police and BTP, LSOA vintage not stated).
- **School quality with mixed Ofsted regimes** [high]: Rank on attainment (KS2 expected standard, Attainment 8, grade 5+ English and maths) plus a binary adverse-outcome flag derived per regime (legacy Inadequate or Requires Improvement; key judgement equivalent; report card Urgent improvement or Needs attention in any core area). Show the actual Ofsted outcome and date verbatim on profile pages. State clearly that proximity is not admission.
  - why: Ofsted says the frameworks are not comparable and there is no composite grade under report cards, so any invented single Ofsted number would be a fabricated fact. About 96% of schools still carried pre-report-card outcomes in March 2026.
  - rejected: Mapping 5-point report card grades onto the old 4-point scale. Claiming catchment membership.
- **OpenStreetMap use** [medium]: Use OSM for water features, cycle tracks and modal filters, but keep OSM-derived tables in a separate schema and be prepared to publish those derived tables under ODbL. Attribute on every map and in the app's About screen. Prefer OGL sources whenever one exists (greenspace, trees, venues).
  - why: OSMF guidance indicates a database of statistics computed from OSM is likely a Derivative Database, which triggers share-alike if publicly used. Isolation keeps the obligation from spreading to proprietary scores.
  - rejected: Avoiding OSM entirely (no comparable open source for current cycle infrastructure). Mixing OSM-derived values into one proprietary table.
- **Refresh cadence** [high]: Monthly: crime, Ofsted MI, GIAS, ODS. Six-monthly: OS Open Greenspace, trees. Annual: Ofcom (November), DfT traffic, performance tables, population estimates, PCM air quality, Cultural Infrastructure Map. Static until reissued: Census 2021, IoD 2025, noise Round 4, flood (as-needed).
  - why: Matches publisher cadences confirmed today and keeps the pipeline cheap.
  - rejected: Live API calls at query time (slow, rate-limited, breaks determinism).

## Risks
- [high] Licence not confirmed for LAEI 2022, Planning London Datahub, Cultural Infrastructure Map 2025 edition, FSA FHRS terms, NHS patients-registered and GP Patient Survey. -> Put the question to GLA, FSA and NHS England, and leave each source out until its licence is settled. Ship v1 on the confirmed fallbacks (PCM 1 km air quality, CIM 2024, IoD GP ratio). Keep a licence register in the repo with the evidence URL and date per source, and block ingestion in CI for any source without an entry.
- [high] Discrimination and steering exposure: ranking areas on demographics, or on proxies such as crime and deprivation, could be framed as digital redlining. User prompts can reveal religion, sexuality or health. -> Feature allowlist enforced in code, not just in the prompt. Protected-characteristic tables never loaded into the ranking store. LLM refusal and redirect pattern for people-based requests. Do not persist raw prompts against accounts by default. Run a DPIA and get a short legal review of Equality Act and UK GDPR exposure before public launch.
- [medium] Ofsted outcomes span three incompatible regimes and most schools have old inspections, so any school quality score can mislead. -> Rank mainly on attainment; use Ofsted only as an adverse flag; always show the verbatim outcome and inspection date; never synthesise an Ofsted grade.
- [medium] ODbL share-alike could attach to Burro's derived feature tables if OSM-derived values are mixed in. -> Separate schema for OSM-derived features, publishable under ODbL on request. Prefer OGL sources. Record provenance per feature.
- [medium] Census 2021 is five years old and was taken during lockdown, which particularly distorts inner London (students, young renters, commuting). -> Use census for slow-moving structure only (tenure, dwelling type, household composition). Do not use travel-to-work. Show the vintage. Use annual ONS population estimates for denominators.
- [medium] Geography vintage mismatches (2011 versus 2021 LSOA and OA) across Ofcom, MPS and older GLA datasets. -> Standardise on 2021 codes and apply ONS best-fit lookups at ingest. Fail the pipeline on unmatched codes above a small threshold.
- [medium] Crime data artefacts: snapped locations, town-centre and station hot spots, varying recording practice, no time of day. -> Residents-plus-workday denominators, shrinkage, category-level display, 24-month windows, and explicit caveats in explanations.
- [medium] Open sources can be withdrawn or go stale (GiGL withdrew in October 2025; TfL CID untouched since 2021). -> Snapshot every source with checksum and retrieval date in object storage. Monitor publisher pages monthly. Design each feature with a named fallback source.
- [medium] Boundary and rollup effects: named neighbourhoods have no official boundaries, and amenities on an edge are miscounted. -> Use distance-decay accessibility from population-weighted centroids rather than in-polygon counts. Version the neighbourhood boundary set.
- [low] Tree and venue datasets have uneven borough coverage, which can masquerade as real differences. -> Compute a per-borough coverage score; down-weight or flag features where coverage is poor; cross-check with OSM.


## Open questions
- Should safety be a rankable dimension at all, or shown only on profile pages? Redfin chose not to show crime data (<https://www.redfin.com/news/neighborhood-crime-data-doesnt-belong-on-real-estate-sites/>).
- Are life-stage preferences (areas with many families, many students) acceptable as positive soft preferences, or should v1 be strictly place-based?
- Will GLA confirm in writing that LAEI 2022 and Planning London Datahub are OGL v3? This decides whether v1 has 20 m air quality and a regeneration dimension.
- Should independent schools be included in school features, and should faith schools be distinguished (religion is a protected characteristic, but school type is a factual attribute)?
- Is the founder willing to publish OSM-derived feature tables under ODbL if required?
- Which source defines the few hundred neighbourhood boundaries? That choice determines the rollup weights and is outside this domain.
- Do the 2025 Ofcom zip files still contain postcode and output-area level files, and on which output area vintage?
- Is a one-off legal review (Equality Act, UK GDPR special category data, App Store review guidelines) in budget before launch?
