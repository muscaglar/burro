# verify:data-licences - Data licences and terms

I checked 18 licence claims against primary sources on 2026-09-23. The open-data foundation holds: 10 claims are confirmed, 5 are partly wrong, 3 were not verified, and none is refuted outright. Nothing found makes the v1 plan illegal, but four corrections change what gets built.

**Corrections that matter most**
- **Small-area rents (major):** the ONS London postcode-district rent workbook has stalled. The latest edition is still April 2025 to March 2026 (published 27 April 2026), and the July 2026 edition is about two months overdue. The GLA, the named user of the series, has moved its Rents Map to borough-level PIPR. Treat the workbook as a frozen snapshot, not a feed.
- **OpenStreetMap and travel times (minor, but the reports contradict each other):** the transit report's mitigation of keeping matrices server-side does not work. ODbL 4.4(c) and 4.6 also bite when a Produced Work from a Derivative Database is shown publicly. The real protections are the OSMF Trivial Transformations guideline, which lists 'a format required for routing', and ODbL 4.6(b), which allows offering the method instead of the database.
- **OSM for QA only (minor):** the OSMF Horizontal Layers guideline says adding non-OSM data 'based on comparisons with OSM data' triggers share-alike. Benchmarking must stay at aggregate level and never correct or gap-fill individual Overture or gazetteer records.
- **Rail timetable bootstrap (major for that step):** the gb-transit prebuilt rail GTFS comes from the RDG feed under the 'Rail Settlement Plan data licence', not OGL. Do not ship travel times built from it until the terms are read.
- **Overture Places (minor):** it is not purely CDLA-Permissive-2.0. Foursquare records are Apache 2.0 and AllThePlaces is CC0, so the CDLA 'Results' clause does not cover the Foursquare share.

**Not verified**
- **Open Parliament Licence text:** not read. The House of Commons Library page confirms use 'in your products' with acknowledgement.
- **BODS data licence:** the BODS site was not read. GOV.UK confirms the BODS legal duty covers England outside London only, so London content must come from TNDS or TfL.
- **NWR Schedule 'Open (OGL3)' label:** Rail Data Marketplace pages were not read. Network Rail's own page says its feeds are under a licence based on OGL.

Limits: no web search was made, and every check opened a known address. No files were downloaded. Pages were read through a reader that summarises, so re-check quoted wording before pasting it into legal documents. No repository files were created or modified.

## Checks
- [confirmed] OGL v3 permits commercial use, requires attribution only, and allows a single linked attributions page when there are many sources.
- [could_not_verify] [impact none] (boundaries) House of Commons Library MSOA Names (v2.3, 13 Feb 2026) are under the Open Parliament Licence, which allows commercial use with acknowledgement.
  - evidence: The House of Commons Library page confirms version 2.3 published 13 February 2026 and says the licence 'allows you to use the MSOA names in your products as long as you acknowledge the source of the information and, where possible, provide a link to the licence'. The licence text itself was not read, at parliament.uk, commonslibrary.parliament.uk/copyright or developer.parliament.uk. The dataset fact is confirmed; the licence wording, exemptions and version are not.
  - correction: No change to the plan. To settle it, open https://www.parliament.uk/site-information/copyright-parliament/open-parliament-licence/ in a browser and save a dated copy in the licence register. MSOA Names are a naming prior, not the gazetteer itself, so exposure is small either way.
- [partly_wrong] [impact minor] (poi-vibe) Overture Maps Places is CDLA-Permissive-2.0, so Burro's aggregates and scores carry no obligations.
  - evidence: Overture's attribution page lists Places sources by licence: Meta, Microsoft, PinMeTo, Krick, RenderSEO, DAC and BrightQuery under 'CDLA Permissive 2.0'; Foursquare under 'Apache 2.0' with a NOTICE requirement; AllThePlaces under 'CC0 1.0'. The Places guide says the theme is 'published under the CDLA Permissive 2.0 and Apache 2.0 licenses, depending on the source' and 'The theme does not include OpenStreetMap data'. Meta supplies about 58 million of about 74 million records. CDLA-Permissive-2.0 section 3.1 is confirmed: 'This agreement does not impose any restriction or obligations with respect to the use, modification, or sharing of Results'. That clause does not exist in Apache 2.0.
  - correction: The report's headline oversimplifies, although its findings table had the detail. All three licences are permissive with no share-alike, so the recommendation stands. Keep the per-record 'sources' property at ingest so records can be filtered by licence, and add the Overture attribution plus the Foursquare Apache 2.0 notice to the attributions page. If place names are ever shown, Foursquare-sourced records carry the Apache notice duty.
- [confirmed] Overture's base, buildings, divisions and transportation themes are ODbL; only Places and Addresses sit outside it.
- [partly_wrong] [impact minor] (poi-vibe, liveability-demographics) OSMF has no guideline on statistical aggregates, and OSMF guidance indicates a table of statistics computed from OSM is likely a Derivative Database.
  - evidence: The OSMF Community Guidelines index lists eight approved guidelines: Regional Cuts, Horizontal Layers, Collective Database, Trivial Transformations, Geocoding, Produced Work, Substantial, and Attribution. None covers statistics, aggregates or analysis results, and the OSMF legal FAQ does not address them either. The first half of the claim is confirmed. The second half is not supported: the Produced Work guideline's only test is 'If the published result of your project is intended for the extraction of the original data, then it is a database and not a Produced Work'. Per-area counts do not allow extraction of the original data, so if anything the test leans towards Produced Work.
  - correction: The poi-vibe wording (a genuine grey area, conservative reading) is accurate. The liveability wording overstates what OSMF says. The cost of the conservative position is low: ODbL 4.6(b) lets Burro offer 'the method of making the alterations to the Database (such as an algorithm)' instead of the table. Keeping OSM-derived features in a separate table remains sensible, but this is not a reason to avoid OSM where it is the only source.
- [partly_wrong] [impact minor] (transit-routing) ODbL share-alike on the travel-time matrix is a low risk that is handled by keeping matrices server-side and attributing OpenStreetMap.
  - evidence: ODbL 4.4(c): 'A Derivative Database is Publicly Used and so must comply with Section 4.4. if a Produced Work created from the Derivative Database is Publicly Used'. ODbL 4.6 applies 'If You Publicly Use a Derivative Database or a Produced Work from a Derivative Database'. So showing travel times on screen counts even if the matrix never leaves the server. What actually protects Burro is the OSMF Trivial Transformations guideline, which lists 'In a format required for routing' as trivial provided 'no other source of data is involved', and says such transformations do 'not add any information that needs to be shared'. The Attribution Guidelines add: 'Applications that incorporate such a routing engine must credit OpenStreetMap'.
  - correction: The risk level (low) is right but the stated mitigation is wrong. Replace it with three steps: build the street network from OSM alone and keep GTFS as a separate all-non-OSM feature type; publish a short methods page naming the OSM extract date, engine version and settings, which would satisfy 4.6(b) if ever needed; credit OpenStreetMap wherever commute times appear, not only on the map. Treating the R5 network (OSM plus GTFS) as covered by the two guidelines is my reading, not an OSMF ruling.
- [partly_wrong] [impact minor] (poi-vibe, boundaries) Using OSM only for internal benchmarking and QA comparison creates no ODbL obligations.
  - evidence: ODbL 4.5 confirms internal use is 'not to the public' and outside share-alike. But the Horizontal Layers guideline lists as share-alike triggers: adding data that improves OSM layers through corrections or additions, and adding non-OSM features 'based on comparisons with OSM data'. The Collective Database guideline gives merging proprietary restaurants with OSM duplicates as a Derivative Database example. The Substantial guideline treats fewer than 100 features as insubstantial but warns 'We regard repeated small extractions as one big extraction!'.
  - correction: Benchmarking is safe only while it produces aggregate quality metrics. It becomes a share-alike problem if OSM is used to drop, add or correct individual Overture records or gazetteer names that then ship. Write this as a rule in the repo conventions: OSM comparison may set a global threshold, never per-record decisions.
- [could_not_verify] [impact minor] (transit-routing) BODS timetable GTFS for the London region is OGL and is the primary input for bus, Tube, DLR and tram.
  - evidence: No page of the BODS site was read, nor the guidance page Transitland cites as the licence URL, nor the DfT Find Transport Data page. Transitland's record for the feed shows commercial use allowed but names no licence. GOV.UK's BODS collection page and implementation guide both say the legal duty covers 'local bus services across England (outside London)' and bus only. The guide does not mention GTFS. The Traveline National Dataset, which is said to feed the BODS GTFS, is listed on data.gov.uk as 'UK Open Government Licence (OGL)', last updated 9 January 2019.
  - correction: Treat London content in the BODS GTFS as unproven on both licence and coverage. BODS does not collect London services by law, so any Tube, DLR, tram or TfL bus data in it comes from TNDS or TfL. The licence-certain source for those modes is TfL's own Journey Planner timetables under the TfL licence. Consider making that the primary input and BODS the cross-check. Settle by downloading the London GTFS and reading the BODS consumer terms in a browser.
- [could_not_verify] [impact major] (transit-routing) The Network Rail schedule on the Rail Data Marketplace is 'Open (OGL3)' and is the licence-safe rail timetable; the gb-transit nightly feed can bootstrap the pipeline.
  - evidence: Rail Data Marketplace product pages were not read, so the 'Open (OGL3)' label was not seen. Network Rail's own page says it encourages use of its data feeds 'provided that it's in compliance with the terms of the Open Government Licence' and its licence page describes a licence 'based on the Open Government Licence'. It also says 'You may not use our brand or logo'. Separately, the gb-transit page states its rail GTFS is built from 'the Rail Delivery Group's DTD feed' under the 'Rail Settlement Plan data licence', and that 'Timetable data is supplied under licence'. The Open Rail Data wiki was not read.
  - correction: The OGL basis for Network Rail data is supported by Network Rail directly, but the Marketplace terms and any Schedule 1 conditions are unread. The gb-transit prebuilt feed is RDG data under a licence nobody on this project has read, so it should not feed anything shown to users. Use it for internal prototyping only. Settle by registering on the Marketplace and saving the accepted terms for the NWR Schedule product.
- [confirmed] TfL open data terms permit commercial use with attribution and contain no clause restricting caching or precomputing.
- [partly_wrong] [impact minor] (housing-cost) HM Land Registry Price Paid Data is OGL v3, with address data restricted; keeping postcode only for aggregation avoids the restriction.
  - evidence: The downloads page confirms OGL v3.0, latest data July 2026, page updated 28 August 2026, and the attribution 'Contains HM Land Registry data (c) Crown copyright and database right 2021. This data is licensed under the Open Government Licence v3.0.' It lists the fields that 'comprise the address data': Postcode, PAON, SAON, Street, Locality, Town/City, District, County. Permitted uses of Address Data are 'for personal and/or non-commercial use' and 'to display for the purpose of providing residential property price information services'. Anything else: 'you must contact Royal Mail'.
  - correction: Postcode is itself Address Data, so dropping the other columns does not take Burro outside the condition. Burro's sale-price layer is plausibly a residential property price information service, which is the permitted commercial use, and aggregating by postcode is standard practice. Risk is low but it rests on that reading. Record the reasoning in the licence register and include it in the pre-launch legal review. Never display or export postcode-level rows.
- [partly_wrong] [impact major] (housing-cost) The ONS 'Private rental market in London' postcode-district workbook exists, is OGL, and is a current quarterly source for small-area rents.
  - evidence: It exists and is OGL: 'Private rental market in London: April 2025 to March 2026', reference 3389, released 27 April 2026, file londonrentalstatsaccessibleq12026.xlsx (129.4 kB), labelled user requested data. ONS search lists nine editions from 23 May 2024 to 27 April 2026, with July editions on 24 July 2024 and 16 July 2025. No edition with a period ending after March 2026 is listed on 23 September 2026. The GLA London Rents Map page now says it uses PIPR, 'published monthly at local authority level', in place of 'the previous ONS user-requested data'. The September 2026 PIPR bulletin makes no mention of small-area London rents.
  - correction: The report flagged this as a risk; the evidence is stronger than that. The July 2026 edition is about two months late and the named user has moved away, so plan as if the series has ended. Archive all nine editions now, design the rent model around the price-ratio prior with the workbook as a dated snapshot, and decide early whether a paid asking-rent source moves from optional calibration to a required input. The report's count of ten editions was nine on the ONS listing.
- [confirmed] The English Indices of Deprivation 2025 were released on 30 October 2025 on 2021 LSOAs under OGL v3, with File 8 giving underlying indicators.
- [confirmed] VOA Council Tax Stock of Properties tables CTSOP3.1 (property type) and CTSOP4.1 (build period) are published at LSOA level under OGL v3.
- [confirmed] Several GLA datasets (LAEI 2022, Cultural Infrastructure Map 2025, Planning London Datahub) show no licence and cannot be assumed OGL.
- [confirmed] PropertyData terms (updated August 2026) allow use inside a customer-facing product on every plan, with a 60-day cache limit, at GBP 28 to 48 per month. Read at <https://propertydata.co.uk/terms> and <https://propertydata.co.uk/api/pricing>.
- [confirmed] Rightmove, Zoopla and OnTheMarket terms each prohibit deep links without consent. Read at <https://www.rightmove.co.uk/c/terms-of-use/>, <https://www.zoopla.co.uk/terms/> and <https://www.onthemarket.com/terms/>.
- [confirmed] Food Standards Agency food hygiene rating data is under the Open Government Licence, with conditions on displaying ratings and imagery.

## New facts
- OS OpenData is confirmed OGL, which clears the boundaries report's unverified flag. The OS open data page says the 18 products are 'available for unrestricted, free commercial and personal reuse under the Open Government Licence (OGL)', and OS documentation says OS Open Names terms 'are provided under the Open Government Licence (OGL) v3' with the exact attribution text in the licence.txt file inside each download.
- ONS geography products need stacked attribution statements. Boundaries need the ONS source line plus 'Contains OS data (c) Crown copyright and database right [year]'. Postcode products (ONSPD, NSPL) add 'Contains Royal Mail data (c) Royal Mail copyright and database right [year]'. UPRN products add a GeoPlace line. Source: https://www.ons.gov.uk/methodology/geography/licences
- In EPC data the postcode is a restricted address field, not OGL. The licensing page lists 'address, address 1, address 2, address 3, postcode' as excluded and says other uses need 'an appropriate licence from Ordnance Survey'. UPRN is OGL. Any future EPC aggregation must geocode by UPRN, not postcode.
- The OSMF Attribution Guidelines say 'Routing engines that use OpenStreetMap data must credit OpenStreetMap. Applications that incorporate such a routing engine must credit OpenStreetMap.' For mobile apps, attribution shown at start-up need not repeat on every screen. So the iOS app needs an OSM credit for commute times even if the basemap were not OSM.
- Overture's September 2026 release had not been published on 23 September 2026 (latest is 2026-08-19.0), but the schema reference already omits the 'categories' property. Burro's first ingest should be written against basic_category and taxonomy from day one.
- TfL's design and branding rules forbid using the roundel, TfL maps or New Johnston font without a licence, and forbid TfL trademarks in app names (examples given include 'Tube map app'). The words 'London Underground', 'Tube', 'DLR' and 'London Overground' are allowed for labelling services and journey planning.
- TfL Journey Planner timetables cover Underground, bus, DLR, tram, cable car and river, not Overground or Elizabeth line. The zip is 24.31 MB, republished every 24 hours, and 'The timetables are updated every 7 days. They do not take account of planned engineering works.' Registration is required.
- The London Datastore site terms ask reusers to state explicitly that the GLA cannot warrant the quality or accuracy of the data, and forbid implying GLA endorsement. This applies to every GLA dataset, including the ones that are OGL, so the attributions page needs that disclaimer.
- PIPR became official statistics on 20 May 2026 and the latest bulletin (16 September 2026) carries August 2026 rents by local authority and bedroom category under OGL v3.0. The borough-level anchor for the rent model is solid.
- HM Land Registry's transaction-to-UPRN lookup was first published on 28 August 2026 under OGL v3.0 and needs two attribution statements (HM Land Registry and OS). The page does not say whether it is backfilled.
- Wikidata structured data in the main, property and lexeme namespaces is CC0; text in other namespaces is CC BY-SA 4.0. The planning.data.gov.uk conservation area dataset is OGL v3.0 with attribution '(c) Historic England 2026. Contains Ordnance Survey data (c) Crown copyright and database right 2026', 10,953 records, and a warning that it is incomplete with duplicates.
- The Ofcom Connected Nations 2025 downloads page lists only three datasets (fixed coverage for local and unitary authorities, fixed performance, mobile coverage) and shows no licence statement or small-area files. The liveability plan's LSOA-level broadband features may have to come from the Indices of Deprivation 2025 broadband indicator instead. I did not open the Ofcom PDFs.
- Four reports take four different positions on OpenStreetMap: boundaries says QA only, poi-vibe says basemap and benchmarking only, liveability says use it for cycle tracks and water in a separate schema, and transit-routing uses it as the street network. These need reconciling into one written policy before any pipeline code is written.
