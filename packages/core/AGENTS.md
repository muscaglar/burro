# packages/core

Burro's ranking engine: the preference spec, the reducer, `rank()`, facts, the verifier and the rule-based interpreter. It is built to [docs/design/contract.md](../../docs/design/contract.md); if the two disagree, change one of them in the same commit.

- Pure Python, no IO. Nothing here opens a file, reads the clock, the environment or a random source, or logs. `tests/test_purity.py` holds the list of imports that are allowed. The only dependency is pydantic.
- `FeatureId` and `TagId` in `ids.py` are the allowlist of ADR 0006. Adding a feature means an enum member, a `FEATURES` row and a row in the contract, in one change. Nothing that describes who lives somewhere gets an id.
- Any change to the arithmetic in `rank.py`, to a rule of the reducer, or to a rule of what an explanation says, bumps `ENGINE_VERSION`. Any change to `FEATURES` or `TAGS` bumps `CATALOGUE_VERSION`. The worked example in `tests/test_rank.py` is the contract's, to four decimals: if it fails, the arithmetic moved.
- A spec changes only through `reducer.apply`. Records are frozen; copy one with `.replace()`, which validates, never with `model_copy(update=...)`, which does not.
- What a person says outweighs what they did not say, and the reducer is where that happens: a mention is worth a half, and the defaults give way to a quarter, rounded down to a step, once a wish is applied, so that one thing said outweighs all that was not. It is written into the spec. `rank()` knows nothing of it and stays a function of the spec and the release.
- What a person takes off stays off. A feature that some tenure weighs by default leaves an entry of 0 behind when it is taken off, which `canonical()`, `check_spec()` and `rank()` pass over. Do not tidy it away: it is what stops a change of tenure bringing the default back.
- **The rule-based reader reads a sentence only when it knows every token in it.** What it knows is a phrase of the lexicon, a name of the release, a number, and the words of `vocabulary.py`. Anything else makes the sentence unknown: no edit, and `other` in `unmet`. There is no list of the words that turn a wish round, because no such list is ever complete. If a wish is read backwards, a word that can turn one is among the plain words: take it out. If a plain wish is not read, a word is missing: add it to `PLAIN` only if it can never turn a wish round, weaken it, compare it, question it or give it to someone else, say why in its group, and move the count in `tests/test_vocabulary.py`. Never add a past tense, a third person or a word that asks. A word that turns needs a rule that accounts for it, and one that no rule accounts for leaves the whole sentence unread.
- A sentence ends at a full stop, `?`, `!` or a line break, and nowhere else. A token is never split at a mark inside it, and a name is never put together across a mark, a hyphen or a line break. A question makes no edit. A sentence that holds doubt and names nothing takes back what the sentences beside it raised.
- Every edit the reader makes says which words it rests on, as offsets in `InterpretResult.rests_on`. Nothing in core keeps the words.
- `tests/test_vocabulary.py` holds the reader to this three ways: the adversary's sentences in `tests/sentences.py`, every thing and every name beside 600 words it does not know, and the share of plain wishes it still reads, which has a floor. The generated test draws a fixed sample in `make ci` and runs in full with `make test ARGS="-m full"`. `WORDS_OF_DOUBT` is not read by the reader: it is there for the test that no plain word is one, and for the model-backed reader.
- A wish that was turned round, "no parks", "not near the shops", never raises a weight. Only a feature in `NUISANCES` is cared about more for being wanted less, and a nuisance that is only named, or that the person says they like, makes no edit: "I like noise".
- The reader takes a name only when the whole of it was typed. Words after "work at" and "30 minutes to" that are the whole of no name are asked about and add no journey. Part of a word is offered by a search and never taken.
- Missing data is never filled in. A value that is not known is `None`, and zero never stands for unknown, except as a sentinel inside `Operations`.
- An error message holds paths, rows and codes, and never a value: not a place id, not a name, not what a person typed. `InterpretResult` and `ExplainInput` have no field that can hold user text.
- A rule of `parse_release` is one small function listed in `RULES`. A new rule needs a row in `BROKEN` in `tests/test_release.py` that shows it refusing a release.
- Every template must pass the verifier, and every fact kind must have a template that is true of it.
- A comparison in a sentence is literally true of the release: the share of areas strictly beyond, rounded down, with the areas that are level counted and said. The mid-rank percentile is for scoring and is never printed in a sentence.
- The verifier is enough for fixed text and not for a model. `explain()` takes `TemplateExplainer` and refuses any other explainer, in code. Do not loosen that check until the verifier is extended as the contract's section 11 says, and do both in one change. Two tests marked as expected to fail hold the sentences the verifier lets through. A test that plants a sentence does it from a subclass of `TemplateExplainer`.
- A reason never states a shortfall: a home over budget and a journey over its cap are never given as a reason, whatever they add to the score. And a trade-off is never something the area does well: it is a component worth less than a half, or a shortfall. An area that does nothing badly has no trade-off.
- Tests run offline and share their releases through `tests/support.py`, imported as `from .support import ...`. Every name in it is made up, and was looked up in an encyclopaedia on 2026-09-23. A name that was found to be real is held in `packages/pipeline/tests/test_synthetic_release.py`, where a test looks for it in every file, the name of a constant included. `tests/test_fixture.py` reads the committed synthetic release, for what must hold of the release that is served. The test reads the bytes; core still opens no file.

## Keeping the contract true

What the code does beyond the first draft of the contract has been written into it: `Release.cutoff`, `InMemoryRelease.documents()`, `Fact.template`, the `station_nearby` template, the names of the refusals that are not one of the ten rules, the clauses a comparison is stated in, and what the rule interpreter does with a plain mention, with a word that turns, with a word it does not know and with a name that has an article. If you add something the contract does not say, write it in where it applies, in the same change.
