## What the workbook says of itself

Read from the sheets of the file that hold words, as text, through the receipt and the licence gate. These are the publisher's words, as the sheets print them. No figure of any area was copied.

Its sheets, in order: Cover Sheet, Contents, Notes, Metadata, Terms and Conditions, Total annual income, Net annual income, Net annual income (equivalised) before housing costs, Net annual income (equivalised) after housing costs, Related Publications.

On the cover:

> To ensure accurate use of the data, please read the notes and metadata first. They include essential details on methodology, limitations, and caveats.

Under "Notes":

> Financial year ending (FYE) 2023 refers to the period between 1st April 2022 to 31st March 2023.

> This workbook uses MSOA codes used by ONS from Census 2021. These are made up of unchanged 2011 MSOAs and new 2021 MSOAs.

> Caution should be applied when interpreting trends over time as the methodology used to produce the estimates is optimised for point-in-time estimates and not for estimating change.

> A confidence interval gives an indication of the degree of uncertainty of an estimate and helps to decide how precise a sample estimate is. It specifies a range of values likely to contain the unknown population value. These values are defined by lower and upper limits. Confidence intervals describe only the uncertainty from sampling; they do not account for all possible errors, and wider intervals indicate less precise estimates that should be interpreted with greater caution.

Under "Metadata":

> These reference tables contain statistics of model-based small area income estimates for financial year ending 2023

> Total annual household income is the sum of the gross income of every member of the household plus any income from benefits such as Working Families Tax Credit.

Under "Terms and Conditions":

> You may re-use this information (not including logos) free of charge in any format or medium, under the terms of the Open Government Licence:

> Users should include a source accreditation to ONS - Source: Office for National Statistics.

## What it does not say

- It states no term that limits reuse beyond the licence.
- It states no level for the confidence interval. The page of quality and methodology is where that is to be read, and nobody has read it in a browser.
- The sentence before the credit names the Land Registry as the source the statistics were adapted from. That is the wording of another of the publisher's workbooks, and is not repeated on any page of Burro.
